# tomtoc Three-Piece Carry System — Full Loadout Review

**Setup reviewed:** TechPack-T73 X-Pac Laptop Backpack 28L · Explorer-T21 X-Pac Sling Bag S · Light-T12 Electronic Accessory Pouch 1.8L
**Electronics evaluated:** ThinkPad T14s Gen 4 · iPhone 12 + AirPods · Anbernic RG40XX-H · 8BitDo SN30 Pro · Canon EOS R50 + Rode VideoMic Go II · Ugreen Nexode 100W GaN charger · Samsung USB-A charger · Ugreen 20,000 mAh + 27,000 mAh power banks · external SSD, USB sticks, cables, AirTag

## 1. TechPack-T73 X-Pac 28L — the hauler

**Build.** X-Pac laminate front over 1680D nylon, YKK AquaGuard zippers, ~47.5 × 31 × 16.5 cm, ~1.3 kg empty, structured semi-rigid frame. Real usable capacity lands between the advertised 28L and reviewers' 25–30L measurements.

**Organization.** Padded tech compartment with suspended, soft-lined sleeve (16" laptop + 13" tablet); front section with quick pockets; top-loading main cavern with document sleeves, zip pockets and a large bottle pocket; luggage pass-through.

**Strengths.** Excellent device protection (high-density padding, suspended sleeve); carries comfortably for its size with plush straps and ventilated back panel; cohesive X-Pac look.

**Weaknesses.** Cumbersome top-loader; front pockets don't open wide; frame doesn't compress on light days.

**How this loadout mitigates it.** The T12 pouch rides at the top of the cavern (one-motion grab for all small electronics); power banks occupy the tablet section against the frame — dense weight near the back, feeding devices through the unzipped pass-through pocket in the laptop section. The AirTag hides in the left-side internal mesh pocket. The cavern then holds the bulk items: Anbernic, camera kit, jacket.

**Score: ★★★★☆** — flaws real, but this configuration works around every one of them.

---

## 2. Explorer-T21 X-Pac Sling Bag S — the on-body layer

**Build.** Matching X-Pac + AquaGuard YKK language, ~31 × 15 × 7.5 cm, ~3L, light enough to forget.

**Organization.** Front quick pocket; main compartment (iPad-Mini class); rear against-the-body pocket that is effectively pickpocket-proof while worn.

**Fit check with this owner's devices.** iPhone 12, AirPods case, small wallet, keys — comfortable with headroom. The rear pocket takes the payment card. On console days the Anbernic RG40XX-H swaps in and still closes. The sling can even carry the SN30 Pro flat for phone-gaming outings without the backpack.

**Strengths.** Never-off crossbody wear fulfills the bar-and-city anti-theft doctrine inherited from four years with a Fjällräven shoulder bag, with better snatch resistance; the reachable bag holds nothing worth stealing.

**Weaknesses.** Non-ambidextrous strap; loose elastic mesh (fixed by assigning every item a home); modest main-compartment padding — this is not a camera bag.

**Score: ★★★★½** — the best piece of the trio.

---

## 3. Light-T12 1.8L — the internal organizer

**Build.** RPET (not X-Pac — irrelevant, it lives inside), dual compartments, stand-up base, sized around a MacBook-class brick.

**Fit check with the actual kit:**
- *Compartment A:* Nexode 100W GaN charger (~71 × 43 × 33 mm, foldable prongs — smaller than a MacBook brick) + Samsung USB-A charger + daily cables. Comfortable.
- *Compartment B:* external SSD, USB sticks, spares — plus the SN30 Pro (~147 × 65 × 25 mm, ~111 g), which fits easily but should ride in a protective shell so nothing compresses its clickable sticks. The owner's cloth wrap prevents scratches but not pressure; a hard EVA case or strict "nothing stacks on the pad" discipline is the real fix.
- *Excluded by design:* both power banks (20,000 mAh at 139 × 80 × 26.5 mm / 420 g; the 27,000 mAh unit similarly) — they'd fill the pouch ~85%+ and belong in the tablet-section charging station instead.

**Margin warning.** With charger, second brick, SSD, sticks, cables and a gamepad aboard, both compartments run near-full. Anyone adding a third brick or both banks should size up to the Navigator-T13 (which tomtoc itself pairs with the T73).

**Score: ★★★★☆** — cures the cavern mess, but 1.8L is a ceiling, not a margin.

## 4. The electronics loadout — how the devices themselves fare

**Device-by-device placement:**

| Device | Home | Fit & protection |
|---|---|---|
| ThinkPad T14s Gen 4 (~1.35 kg) | Suspended sleeve, tech compartment | Excellent — the sleeve exists for exactly this class of 14" ultrabook |
| Power banks 20k + 27k | Tablet section + pass-through cable pocket | The purpose-built charging station; correct weight placement |
| Nexode 100W + Samsung chargers, cables, SSD, sticks | T12 compartments A/B | Near-full but organized; one lift-out unit |
| 8BitDo SN30 Pro | T12, in a hard shell | Fine with shell; unprotected sticks are the only fragility |
| Anbernic RG40XX-H | Main cavern (or T21 sling on console days) | Padded against the T73's structure; handheld fits the sling too |
| Canon EOS R50 + Rode VideoMic Go II | Main cavern, wrapped | **The system's weak point — see below** |
| iPhone 12, AirPods, wallet, keys, card | T21 S sling | Ideal; card in rear against-body pocket |
| AirTag | T73 left internal mesh pocket | Hidden, stable, away from grab zones |

**Weight estimate for the full loadout:** roughly 3.5 kg of devices and accessories plus ~1.3 kg backpack and ~0.3 kg sling → **about 5–5.5 kg all-in**. That is well within the T73's comfortable carry envelope — the plush straps and back panel earn their keep here — and on days without the camera kit the load drops to ~4 kg.

**The camera problem.** The R50 (~375 g body) plus the VideoMic Go II is the only substantial gear with no dedicated, padded home. Wrapped in cloth in the cavern it survives commuting, but the T21 S cannot swallow camera-plus-mic, and the cavern offers no structure. Two honest upgrades, in order of cost: a simple padded camera insert/pouch inside the main cavern (keeps the three-bag system intact), or on dedicated shoot days, tomtoc's 7L Explorer-T21 M sling, which fits an 11" tablet-class device and would take camera + mic properly. Neither is required for the system to work today — but the R50 is the item the current setup protects least.

**Charging coverage — a genuine strength.** Between the Nexode 100W (laptop-class wall charging), both banks (up to 100W output), the pass-through routing and the spare cables, this loadout can charge laptop, phone, handheld and gamepad simultaneously from wall or battery. Few 28L setups achieve true "self-powered workstation" status; this one does, and every component has a fixed address.

**Redundancy audit.** Almost none: one wall charger per plug class, one SSD, one pad — no duplicate pockets fighting for the same job. The only overlapping capability is the two power banks, justified by differing capacities/output rather than duplication.

---

## Final assessment

**Who this exact system suits:** the hybrid commuter-creator — laptop-first days with occasional camera, handheld and game sessions — who values device protection, coherent materials, disciplined anti-theft carry and self-sufficiency in charging above minimalism.

**System + loadout score: ★★★★☆** (half a star withheld for the unprotected camera cell and the pouch's tight margins). The bag trio is ★★★★½; the integration of the actual electronics is what earns the system its coherence — and the camera insert is the single upgrade that would push the whole loadout to full marks.
